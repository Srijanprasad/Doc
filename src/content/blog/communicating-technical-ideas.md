Technical work is easier to understand when the story starts with the problem, not the tool. Before describing an implementation, explain who it helps and what was difficult before it existed.

## Start with the context

Give readers enough background to understand the constraints. A short explanation of the user, the workflow, and the limitation is usually more useful than a long list of technologies.

## Explain the trade-offs

For each important decision, describe the alternatives you considered and why one fit the situation better. This makes the reasoning reusable, even when a reader's tools are different.

## Close with the result

Summarize what changed, what you learned, and what you would improve next. Keep claims measurable where possible, and distinguish verified results from future plans.

> Clear technical writing is part of building a dependable product: it helps people review, use, and maintain the work.
